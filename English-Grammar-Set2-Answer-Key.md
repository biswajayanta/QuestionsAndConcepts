## UNIT 1 — DETERMINERS

(a) **an** — "honest" starts with a vowel sound (silent h).
(b) **few** — negative sense: he's new (joined last month), so not many friends yet — the "but" that follows ("but the few he has are close ones") confirms the contrast with a small number.
(c) **the** — "the same time," a specific, already-understood reference.
(d) **much** — "money" is uncountable.
(e) **a little** — positive sense: he genuinely does give something, however small, to charity.
(f) **Many** — the sentence structure sets up a contrast ("Many...admire him...though a few think...") — most admire him, a smaller group disagrees.
(g) **an** — "old" starts with a vowel sound.
(h) **This** — demonstrative referring back to the specific act just described.
(i) **an** — "engineer" starts with a vowel sound.
(j) **an** — "hour" starts with a silent h → vowel sound.

## UNIT 2 — TENSES

(a) **organises** — simple present, habitual/yearly action.
(b) **will take place** (or "takes place," schedule-present is also acceptable) — future scheduled event, "next Friday."
(c) **have already started** — present perfect, recent action with present relevance.
(d) **was training** — past continuous, the ongoing background action.
(e) **started** — simple past, the interrupting action.
(f) **had** (as in "had to be cancelled") — simple past, a direct consequence within the same past timeline.
(g) **had left** — past perfect, this happened *before* another past event (the rain stopping).
(h) **has participated** — present perfect, started in the past (Class 6) and continues to now.
(i) **has won** — present perfect, an unspecified number of times "so far," up to now.
(j) **hopes** — simple present, a current intention/state.

---

## UNIT 3 — MODALS

(a) **must** — strong obligation, a firm school rule.
(b) **should** — advice/moral obligation (not a strict requirement, note the contrast "though they are not required...").
(c) **must not** — strict prohibition, safety rule.
(d) **May** — polite request for permission.
(e) **must not** — strict prohibition (exam rules).
(f) **may/might** — possibility, genuinely uncertain ("the school hasn't decided yet").
(g) **could** — past general ability.
(h) **Would/Could** — polite request.

## UNIT 4 — SUBJECT–VERB CONCORD (editing)

| # | Incorrect | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | have | **has** | "Each" is always singular, regardless of "of the players." |
| 2 | are | **is** | "Team" treated as one unit (a single group of selectors meeting together). |
| 3 | was | **were** | "Neither...nor" agreement matches the nearer subject — "his assistants" (plural). |
| 4 | have | **has** | "The number of" (a specific count) takes a singular verb. |
| 5 | was | **were** | "A number of" (meaning "many") takes a plural verb. |
| 6 | are | **is** | "Along with his two sons" is an inserted phrase; real subject is "my uncle" (singular). |
| 7 | were | **was** | "Everybody" is an indefinite pronoun, always singular. |
| 8 | are | **is** | Real subject is "the list" (singular); "of winners" is a prepositional phrase. |
| 9 | seem | **seems** | "Either" is always singular. |
| 10 | have | **has** | "The jury" as a single unit reaching one unanimous decision. |

## UNIT 5 — REPORTED SPEECH

**Part A — model completion:**

(a) **why he had been absent the day before** *(Wh-question → no "if/whether," statement word order; "yesterday" → "the day before," since the teacher is asking about a day now in the past relative to when she's asking)*

(b) **he had not been well, Ma'am** *(simple past "was not well" → backshifts to past perfect "had not been well")*

(c) **he had had a high fever** *(simple past "had" [main verb, meaning "suffered from"] → backshifts to past perfect "had had" — this double "had" often trips students up, but it's correct: one "had" for the backshift, one "had" as the original main verb)*

(d) **he should have informed the school office** *("should have + past participle" is already a fixed past-modal form expressing an unfulfilled past obligation — it does not backshift any further)*

(e) **he would inform them next time** *("will" → "would"; "next time" doesn't need a time-word change here since it's a general future reference, not tied to a specific date like "tomorrow")*

(f) **to submit a medical certificate the next day** *(request → "requested + object + to + infinitive"; "tomorrow" → "the next day")*

(g) **he would bring it without fail** *("will" → "would")*

**Part B — model answer (his wording can differ, but check for these elements):**

> The mother asked Riya where she had kept her umbrella. Riya replied that she had kept it in the hall cupboard. The mother requested her to bring it to her, adding that it looked like it was going to rain. Riya agreed and said that she would bring it right away. The mother then reminded her not to forget to take her raincoat as well when she left for school. Riya assured her that she would not forget and thanked her for reminding her.

**Check his version against these specific points:**
- "have you kept" (present perfect) → "had kept" (past perfect) ✓
- "kept it" (simple past) → "had kept it" (past perfect) ✓
- "Please bring it to me" (request) → "requested her to bring it to her" — NOT "told her" (it's a polite request, signalled by "please")
- "it looks like" (present simple) → "it looked like" (past simple)
- "it's going to rain" (present continuous) → "it was going to rain" (past continuous)
- "I will bring it" → "she would bring it" (will → would)
- "Don't forget..." (negative command) → "reminded/told her not to forget..." ("not to + infinitive")
- "when you leave for school" (present simple) → "when she left for school" (past simple)
- "I won't forget" → "she would not forget"

## Quick note for you on grading this set

Since this set mirrors the real exam format, the most useful thing to check is whether he's applying the **backshift rule** consistently across Units 2 and 5 (tenses, and reported speech) — that was the one pattern flagged as still developing in the last set. If Part B of Unit 5 (the full paragraph, no blanks to guide him) comes out mostly correct on his own, that's a strong sign the rule has properly landed rather than just being followed when the blank structure hints at it.
