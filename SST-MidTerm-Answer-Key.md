## GEOGRAPHY

1. **Community-owned resources** (resources accessible to the whole community, e.g., public parks, playgrounds, roads).
2. **1987** (the National Water Policy of 1987, later revised in 2002 and 2012).
3. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.**
4. **Ferrous:** iron ore (or manganese, chromite). **Non-ferrous:** copper (or bauxite, tin).

5. **Renewable:** solar energy, wind energy, forests, water (examples) — can be replenished/regenerated naturally over a relatively short time. **Non-renewable:** coal, petroleum/minerals (examples) — take millions of years to form and exist in a finite quantity. Resource planning is essential because resources are unevenly distributed, and indiscriminate use without planning leads to resource depletion, socio-economic disparities, and environmental degradation — planning ensures resources are used judiciously for balanced, sustainable development across regions.

6. **Any three, e.g.:** (i) Deforestation for agricultural expansion, industry, or mining removes vegetation cover, exposing soil to erosion; (ii) overgrazing by livestock degrades grasslands and vegetation cover; (iii) mining activities leave large tracts of degraded land, with overburden/waste dumped on the surrounding land; (iv) over-irrigation in some areas leads to waterlogging and soil salinity, rendering land unfit for cultivation.

7. **Rabi crops:** sown in winter (October–December), harvested in summer (April–June); examples: wheat, gram, mustard. **Kharif crops:** sown with the onset of monsoon (June–July), harvested in autumn (September–October); examples: rice, maize, cotton.

8. **Any five, e.g.:** (i) Creation of national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, and biosphere reserves to protect habitats; (ii) special Project Tiger-type conservation programmes for specific endangered species; (iii) afforestation and social/community forestry programmes; (iv) involving local communities in conservation through joint forest management, since local people often have the strongest stake in sustainable use; (v) legal protection through Acts (e.g., Wildlife Protection Act) banning hunting/poaching and regulating trade in wildlife products; (vi) creating awareness through education about the ecological value of biodiversity.

9. **Key points:** India isn't short of water in absolute terms overall, but water scarcity arises because: (i) water is unevenly distributed — some regions have abundant rainfall, others very little; (ii) rapid population growth and expanding agriculture/industry have massively increased demand, causing over-exploitation of groundwater and surface sources; (iii) access is unequal — wealthier households/regions/industries often secure disproportionate water access, leaving poorer communities with scarce or contaminated supply; (iv) pollution of existing water sources (industrial effluents, sewage) further reduces the amount of usable water, compounding the scarcity even where water physically exists.

10. **Key points:** Iron ore is one of India's most important minerals — India has fairly large reserves, concentrated mainly in Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, and Karnataka (the Chhotanagpur plateau region is especially rich). It's the basic raw material for the iron and steel industry, crucial for infrastructure, machinery, and manufacturing — making these belts significant industrial regions of the country.

## CIVICS / POLITICAL SCIENCE

1. **The Dutch-speaking (Flemish) and French-speaking (Walloon) communities.**

4. **Any one:** Indian National Congress, Bharatiya Janata Party, Bahujan Samaj Party, Communist Party of India (Marxist), Nationalist Congress Party, etc.

6. **Federal:** power is constitutionally divided between a central government and regional/state governments, each with independent authority in their own spheres (e.g., India, USA). **Unitary:** all power rests with the central/national government, which may delegate some power to lower levels but can also overrule them (e.g., Sri Lanka, United Kingdom in the classical sense).

7. **Any three, e.g.:** (i) Dynastic succession — leadership positions in many parties tend to stay within a family, limiting fair opportunities for others; (ii) growing role of money and muscle power in elections, especially from wealthy or influential candidates; (iii) lack of meaningful internal democracy — ordinary members often have little real say in choosing leaders or shaping policies; (iv) parties sometimes fail to offer genuinely different choices to voters on major issues, reducing meaningful political alternatives.

8. **Prudential reasons:** power-sharing helps reduce the possibility of conflict between social groups, since groups that feel excluded from power often resort to violent conflict; it also makes decisions more durable and widely accepted since more stakeholders were involved. **Moral reasons:** power-sharing is the very spirit of democracy — a democratic rule involves sharing power with those affected by its exercise, and people have a right to be consulted on how they are governed, regardless of whether it also happens to reduce conflict. Together, these show power-sharing isn't just a strategic necessity to avoid conflict — it's a valuable good in itself for a healthy democracy.

9. **Any five outcomes, e.g.:** (i) Democracy establishes legitimate government, accountable to the people; (ii) it promotes equality and dignity for citizens, at least in principle and increasingly in practice; (iii) it improves the quality of decision-making through deliberation and public debate; (iv) it provides a method for peaceful conflict resolution and orderly transfer of power through elections; (v) it allows for correction of its own mistakes over time, since governments can be voted out. **One limitation:** economic inequality and disparities often persist even under democratic rule — formal political equality doesn't automatically translate into economic or social equality.

10. **Federal features:** a written Constitution as the supreme law; a clear division of powers between the Centre and states (Union, State, and Concurrent lists); an independent judiciary to resolve Centre-state disputes; states have their own elected governments with defined powers. **Two centralising/unitary-leaning features:** (i) a single Constitution and citizenship for the whole country, with no separate state constitutions; (ii) Parliament's power to alter state boundaries or reorganise/create states through ordinary legislation, without needing the affected state's consent.

## ECONOMICS

1. **Primary sector.**
2. **Per capita income.**
3. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.**
4. **World Trade Organisation (WTO).**

5. **Any three limitations, e.g.:** (i) Per capita income is an average — it doesn't show how income is actually distributed among the population, so it can mask significant inequality; (ii) it ignores other essential aspects of development like health, education, and access to basic services/infrastructure; (iii) it doesn't capture non-monetary/non-market aspects of well-being, such as environmental quality, personal security, or gender equality.

6. **Organised sector:** enterprises registered with the government, following fixed rules (working hours, minimum wages, job security, social security benefits) — e.g., government jobs, employees of registered companies/factories. **Unorganised sector:** small, often unregistered enterprises with no job security or fixed working conditions/benefits — e.g., street vendors, small farmers, casual construction labourers.

7. **Any three, e.g.:** (i) Indian producers/companies now face greater competition from foreign goods and MNCs, which has forced some local producers (especially in sectors like toys, batteries) out of business; (ii) but it has also created new opportunities/jobs for some Indian producers who can supply raw materials or services to MNCs, or export competitively (e.g., IT/BPO sector); (iii) for workers, especially in the unorganised sector, globalisation has often meant more flexible/insecure employment conditions, with limited job security or benefits, even as some formal-sector jobs have grown in export-oriented industries.

8. **Formal sources:** banks and cooperative societies, regulated by the RBI, with transparent, generally lower interest rates and legal recourse for borrowers. **Informal sources:** moneylenders, traders, employers, relatives — largely unregulated, often charging very high interest rates with no oversight, which can trap poor borrowers in escalating debt. The government/RBI wants to expand formal credit access because it protects borrowers from exploitative terms and debt traps, and ensures credit actually supports productive investment (e.g., in farming or small business) rather than deepening poverty.

9. **Key points:** Historically, a very large share of India's workforce was in the primary (agricultural) sector, but over time it has been declining relative to the secondary (industry) and tertiary (services) sectors. Reasons: agriculture has limited capacity to absorb an increasing workforce beyond a point (disguised unemployment on farms is common); growth in industry and especially services (IT, banking, trade, tourism) has created new job opportunities; economic development typically comes with rising incomes and rising demand for goods and services, which further fuels growth in secondary/tertiary sectors. This shift, generally, is a marker of economic development, though it needs to be accompanied by genuinely productive job creation in the non-agricultural sectors to be a truly positive sign, rather than people being pushed out of agriculture with no good alternative (a concern in parts of India).

10. **Key points:** MNCs are companies that own or control production in more than one country, and they play a central role in globalisation by: (i) setting up production units/factories in countries (like India) where costs (labour, raw materials) are lower; (ii) placing orders with local small producers to manufacture goods according to MNC specifications, which are then branded and sold under the MNC's own label globally; (iii) buying out or partnering with local companies to expand their production/market reach; (iv) investing capital and bringing in new technology and production methods, which spreads specific standards and practices globally, closely linking economies together.

## Quick scoring guide for you

- 1-mark questions: exact/near-exact match to key.
- 3-mark questions: needs at least the number of distinct points asked for (e.g., "any three") — check for genuinely separate reasons, not the same point restated.
- 5-mark questions: check both breadth (enough distinct points) and whether he actually explains each point rather than just listing it — CBSE SST long-answers are scored on explanation, not just naming.
- The assertion-reason questions here are genuinely CBSE-pattern for Social Science (unlike English), so no need to skip these.
