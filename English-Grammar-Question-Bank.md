## UNIT 1 — DETERMINERS (18 questions)

**A. Fill in the blank with the correct determiner (choose from the options):**

1. She is _____ honest person. (a / an / the)
2. I need _____ information about the new syllabus. (much / many / a few)
3. There are only _____ chances left to register — hurry! (a few / few / much)
4. He has _____ friends in this city; he feels quite lonely. (a few / few / a little)
5. _____ student must submit the form by Friday. (Each / All / Some)
6. _____ of the two brothers is a doctor. (Each / Every / Both)
7. _____ sun rises in _____ east. (A/an, an / The, the / A, the)
8. I saw _____ European delegate at the conference. (a / an / the)
9. There isn't _____ water left in the bottle. (many / much / few)
10. She bought _____ umbrella yesterday, and _____ umbrella was blue. (a, the / an, the / the, a)
11. _____ children in the class scored above 90% — most did well. (Many / Much / Little)
12. He gave me _____ advice, and it was genuinely useful. (a little / little / few)
13. _____ hour of waiting felt like a whole day. (A / An / The)
14. _____ boy in the row must stand for the anthem, not just some of them. (Every / Both / Either)
15. I have _____ money left — barely enough for the bus fare. (a little / little / a few)

**B. Assertion–Reason:**

16. **Assertion (A):** "Few" and "a few" mean exactly the same thing and can be used interchangeably.
**Reason (R):** Both refer to a countable plural noun in a negative sense.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A. (c) A is false, but R is true. (d) A is true, but R is false.

17. **Assertion (A):** We use "an" before "university" even though it starts with a vowel letter.
**Reason (R):** The choice between "a" and "an" depends on the sound at the start of the word, not the letter — "university" begins with a "yoo" (consonant) sound.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A. (c) A is true, but R is false. (d) Both A and R are false.

18. **Assertion (A):** "Each" can be used for a group of only two people.
**Reason (R):** "Each" emphasizes the individuality of members within a group, and can apply to two or more.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A. (c) A is true, but R is false. (d) A is false, but R is true.

## UNIT 2 — TENSES (18 questions)

**A. Fill in the blank with the correct form of the verb given in brackets:**

1. The sun _____ (rise) in the east.
2. Look! The children _____ (play) in the garden right now.
3. She _____ (finish) her homework already; she can go out now.
4. He _____ (live) in Bengaluru for ten years now, and still does.
5. They _____ (leave) for Delhi tomorrow morning; the tickets are booked.
6. When I reached the station, the train _____ (already/leave).
7. While she _____ (cook) dinner, the phone rang.
8. I _____ (not/see) him since last month.
9. He _____ (go) to the market yesterday and _____ (buy) some vegetables.
10. By the time the guests arrived, she _____ (finish) cooking.
11. She _____ (study) English for five years now.
12. I _____ (meet) him at the mall last week.

**B. Choose the correct option:**

13. Which sentence correctly uses the present perfect tense?
(a) I saw that movie already. (b) I have seen that movie already. (c) I am seeing that movie already. (d) I see that movie already.

14. Identify the correctly formed past perfect sentence:
(a) She had left before he arrives. (b) She had left before he arrived. (c) She has left before he arrived. (d) She left before he had arrived.

15. Choose the sentence with correct use of "since" and "for":
(a) I have lived here for 2010. (b) I have lived here since five years. (c) I have lived here since 2010. (d) I have lived here for 2010 years.

**C. Assertion–Reason:**

16. **Assertion (A):** "I have finished my homework" and "I finished my homework yesterday" can both be used interchangeably in all contexts.
**Reason (R):** Present perfect tense requires an unspecified time or present relevance, while simple past tense requires a specific past time marker.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. (b) A is false, but R is true, and R explains why A is false. (c) Both A and R are false. (d) A is true, but R is false.

17. **Assertion (A):** "He was going to the market when it started raining" uses the past continuous tense to describe two simultaneous or interrupted past actions.
**Reason (R):** Past continuous tense is used for an ongoing action in the past that is interrupted by another action, or for two actions happening at the same time in the past.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A. (c) A is true, but R is false. (d) Both A and R are false.

18. **Assertion (A):** Past perfect tense is used to show which of two past actions happened earlier.
**Reason (R):** Past perfect is formed using "had + past participle."
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A. (c) A is true, but R is false. (d) A is false, but R is true.

## UNIT 3 — MODALS (18 questions)

**A. Fill in the blank with the correct modal:**

1. You _____ wear a helmet while riding a bike — it's the law. (must / might / could)
2. You _____ come to the party if you don't want to; it's entirely optional. (must not / need not / cannot)
3. You _____ smoke inside the hospital — it is strictly forbidden. (need not / must not / may not always)
4. _____ I borrow your pen for a moment? (May / Must / Should)
5. It _____ rain later today; the sky looks a bit cloudy. (must / might / need)
6. When I was young, I _____ run for hours without getting tired. (could / must / should have)
7. You _____ apologise to her — it's simply good manners. (should / need not / must not)
8. I _____ to submit this report by 5 pm; it's a strict company deadline. (must / have / should)
9. _____ you please pass the salt? (Would / Must / Shall)
10. She _____ speak three languages fluently. (can / must / should)

**B. Choose the option that correctly distinguishes meaning:**

11. Which sentence means "it is not necessary"?
(a) You must not go. (b) You need not go. (c) You may not go. (d) You cannot go.

12. Which sentence means "it is forbidden"?
(a) You need not smoke here. (b) You must not smoke here. (c) You may smoke here. (d) You could smoke here.

13. Choose the sentence expressing strong obligation based on the speaker's personal judgment (not an external rule):
(a) I have to wear a uniform to school. (b) I must finish this task tonight, I feel it's important. (c) I need to submit the form by the deadline. (d) I may finish this task tonight.

**C. Assertion–Reason:**

14. **Assertion (A):** "You must not park here" and "You need not park here" convey the same meaning.
**Reason (R):** "Must not" expresses prohibition, while "need not" expresses absence of obligation — these are very different meanings.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R explains A. (b) A is false, but R is true, and R explains why A is false. (c) Both A and R are false. (d) A is true, but R is false.

15. **Assertion (A):** "Have to" is generally used for obligation arising from external circumstances or rules, while "must" often expresses the speaker's own sense of obligation.
**Reason (R):** Both "must" and "have to" always refer to exactly the same source of obligation.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R explains A. (b) A is true, but R is false. (c) A is false, but R is true. (d) Both A and R are false.

16. **Assertion (A):** "May" and "might" both express possibility, but "might" usually indicates a lower degree of certainty than "may."
**Reason (R):** "Might" is simply the past tense form of "may" and cannot be used to express present possibility.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R explains A. (b) A is true, but R is false. (c) A is false, but R is true. (d) Both A and R are false.

**D. Identify and correct the error in modal usage:**

17. "You needn't to worry about the exam; you have prepared well."
18. "She can able to solve this puzzle easily."

## UNIT 4 — SUBJECT–VERB CONCORD (20 questions)

**A. Choose the correct verb form:**

1. Each of the students _____ (has/have) submitted the assignment.
2. The team _____ (is/are) practising hard for the tournament, playing as one unit.
3. Neither the manager nor the employees _____ (was/were) present at the meeting.
4. The captain, along with his teammates, _____ (was/were) felicitated.
5. A number of students _____ (was/were) absent today.
6. The number of students in the class _____ (is/are) forty.
7. Everyone in the hall _____ (was/were) asked to remain silent.
8. Bread and butter _____ (is/are) my favourite breakfast.
9. Ram and Shyam _____ (is/are) close friends.
10. Neither of the answers _____ (is/are) correct.
11. Either the students or the teacher _____ (is/are) responsible for this mess.
12. The jury _____ (has/have) given its verdict — they were unanimous as one body.
13. My brother, as well as my parents, _____ (is/are) coming to the function.
14. Nobody _____ (know/knows) the answer to this question.
15. The news of his success _____ (was/were) surprising to everyone.

**B. Identify the error in subject-verb agreement and correct it:**

16. "The list of items are on the table."
17. "Either of the two roads lead to the station."
18. "The committee have decided to postpone the event." (correct if referring to the committee as a single unit)

**C. Assertion–Reason:**

19. **Assertion (A):** In the sentence "The captain, along with his teammates, was honoured," the verb "was" (singular) is correct.
**Reason (R):** Phrases like "along with," "as well as," and "together with" do not change the number of the subject; the verb still agrees with the main subject alone.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R explains A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A. (c) A is true, but R is false. (d) A is false, but R is true.

20. **Assertion (A):** In "Neither the teacher nor the students were present," the verb "were" agrees with "students" because it is the subject nearer to the verb.
**Reason (R):** In sentences joined by "either...or" or "neither...nor," the verb always agrees with the first-mentioned subject, regardless of position.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R explains A. (b) A is true, but R is false — the verb agrees with the nearer subject, not the first one. (c) A is false, but R is true. (d) Both A and R are false.

## UNIT 5 — REPORTED SPEECH: Statements, Questions, Commands, Requests (20 questions)

**A. Convert the following into reported/indirect speech:**

1. He said, "I am going to the market."
2. She said, "I have completed my project."
3. The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."
4. He said to me, "I will help you tomorrow."
5. She said, "I was reading a novel when you called."
6. He asked, "Are you coming to the party?"
7. She asked me, "Do you like ice cream?"
8. The teacher asked the students, "Where do you live?"
9. He asked her, "What is your name?"
10. She asked, "Why are you late today?"
11. The officer said, "Sit down!"
12. The mother said to her child, "Don't touch that wire."
13. He said to the waiter, "Please bring me a glass of water."
14. The teacher said to the students, "Submit your assignments on time."
15. She said, "Please don't shout in the library."

**B. Identify and correct the error in the reported speech below:**

16. Direct: He said, "Where are you going?" — Incorrect reported version: "He asked that where was I going."
17. Direct: She asked, "Are you free tomorrow?" — Incorrect reported version: "She asked if am I free the next day."
18. Direct: The teacher said, "Do your homework daily." — Incorrect reported version: "The teacher said do your homework daily."

**C. Assertion–Reason:**

19. **Assertion (A):** While reporting a yes/no question, we use "if" or "whether," and the clause follows statement word order, not question word order.
**Reason (R):** In reported speech, questions lose their original interrogative structure and are expressed as a statement embedded within the reporting sentence.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R explains A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A. (c) A is true, but R is false. (d) A is false, but R is true.

20. **Assertion (A):** When converting a command into reported speech, we use "to + infinitive" after the reporting verb (e.g., "ordered/told him to leave").
**Reason (R):** Commands in reported speech always retain the exact imperative verb form used in the original direct speech.
Options: (a) Both A and R are true, and R explains A. (b) A is true, but R is false. (c) A is false, but R is true. (d) Both A and R are false.
