# JEE Foundation Program — Master Plan

**Started:** Class 10, mid-year (CBSE, NPS Kudlu)
**Goal:** Build genuine conceptual depth, precise reading/comprehension of tricky problem statements, and correct problem-solving technique — not just "more practice." Sequenced from Class 10 fundamentals into 11th/12th JEE Main + Advanced level.

---

## How this system works

1. **Curated sets, not random drills.** Each "Set" has a small number of questions (starting at 10, moving to 3–5/day once the rhythm is set) that are *logically sequenced* — each question either builds on the previous one's concept or deliberately re-tests a concept from a different angle.
2. **Questions are JEE-foundation/Olympiad-flavoured**, meaning: the wording is intentionally not a direct formula plug-in. He has to read carefully, identify what's actually being asked, decide *which* concept applies, and only then compute.
3. **Two separate documents per set:**
   - `SetNN-Questions.md` — only the questions. Give **only this file** to your son.
   - `SetNN-Concepts-and-Solutions.md` — the underlying concept/theorem for each question, common traps, the fully correct method, and the final answer. **Keep this for yourself** to check his work against.
4. **Workflow:**
   - Give him the Questions file (or read them out).
   - He attempts them on paper, showing full working.
   - You check his working against the Concepts & Solutions doc — not just the final answer, but *the method*.
   - If correct: note it, move on.
   - If wrong or shaky: walk him through the "Correct Approach" section in his own words, then (if needed) give him a near-identical follow-up question later in the week to re-test the same concept.
5. **You send me his responses (correct, wrong, partially correct, or his exact working) after each set.** I'll use that to:
   - Decide what the *next* set should target (repeat a weak concept vs. move forward),
   - Adjust difficulty up or down,
   - Start tagging his specific weak spots over time so the whole program becomes personalized rather than generic.

This document itself will stay updated as the single source of truth for where we are in the roadmap.

## Roadmap (high level)

### Phase 1 — Class 10, now through boards (current)
Consolidate and stress-test topics as they're covered in school/Allen, in this rough order (matches your son's current coverage):

- **Maths:** Real Numbers → Polynomials → Pair of Linear Equations → Quadratic Equations → Arithmetic Progressions → Triangles → Coordinate Geometry → Trigonometry (intro + applications) → Circles → Mensuration → Statistics & Probability
- **Physics:** Light (Reflection & Refraction, lenses, mirrors) → Human Eye & Optical Instruments → Electricity → Magnetic Effects of Current
- **Chemistry:** Chemical Reactions & Equations → Acids/Bases/Salts → Metals & Non-metals → Carbon & Its Compounds → Periodic Classification
- **Biology:** Life Processes → Control & Coordination → Reproduction → Heredity & Evolution → Our Environment
- **SST:** History (Nationalism in Europe → Nationalism in India → Age of Industrialisation → Print Culture), Geography (Resources, Agriculture, Minerals & Energy, Manufacturing, Lifelines of Economy), Civics (Power Sharing, Federalism, Political Parties), Economics (Development, Sectors, Money & Credit, Globalisation)

Even at this stage, Maths/Physics/Chemistry questions will be pitched at JEE-foundation level (i.e., harder than school exams need, at the level Allen or NTSE/Olympiad would expect), since the goal is early conceptual compounding.

### Phase 2 — Class 11 (once boards are done)
Shift fully to JEE syllabus sequencing: Sets, Functions, Trigonometry, Quadratic Equations, Sequences & Series, Straight Lines & Conic Sections (Maths); Mechanics, Thermodynamics (Physics); Mole Concept, Atomic Structure, Chemical Bonding, Equilibrium (Chemistry). SST drops out of scope here unless you want it retained for board purposes.

### Phase 3 — Class 12
Calculus-heavy Maths, Electrodynamics/Modern Physics, Organic Chemistry, Coordination Compounds — full JEE Main + Advanced level, mixed with previous years' actual JEE questions.

### What Set 01 taught us about his specific weak spots (feeding into Set 02 and beyond)
- **Carrying a derived formula through to the numeric step**: on M2 he proved the general formula correctly but initially reverted to a simpler/wrong relation when plugging in numbers. Watch for this pattern generally — he can derive but sometimes doesn't trust/reuse his own result.
- **cot θ vs tan θ vs sin θ/cos θ confusion**: he briefly substituted sinθ/cosθ values in place of cotθ/tanθ. Worth drilling until automatic.
- **Opposite vs adjacent side identification**: on P1 he initially assigned the wrong side to the wrong trig ratio (fixed on retry — but worth occasional spot checks).
- **Overclaiming from limited data (the C2 pattern)**: on the pH/neutralisation question he initially claimed "equal strength" when the data only supported "equal moles of H+/OH- reacted." This is a recurring, important JEE-comprehension trap — re-tested in Set 02 with new numbers.
- **SST answers tend to state facts without the underlying "economic/political logic"**: H1 and H2 in Set 01 needed a revision round to add real reasoning rather than just listing what happened. Set 02 pushes this further.
- **Genuine strengths**: he independently found and used the "test all cases" approach on M1 (checking every prime value systematically) and correctly identified both real/virtual cases on P2 (mirror magnification) unprompted — both are strong signs of real conceptual grip, not just procedure-following.
