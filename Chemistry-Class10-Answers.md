### Very short answer (2 marks)

**K8.** (2 marks)
Electron dot structure (each ':' is one shared pair): [1]
```
      H
      :
  H : C : H
      :
      H
```
Carbon has 4 valence electrons. Losing 4 electrons needs too much energy, and gaining 4 would put 10 electrons round 6 protons, which cannot hold them. So carbon **shares** electrons and forms covalent bonds. [1]

**K9.** (2 marks)
A **homologous series** is a family of compounds with the **same functional group**, the **same general formula** and similar chemical properties, in which successive members differ by **–CH2–**. [1]
First four alkanes: **CH4, C2H6, C3H8, C4H10**. [1]

**K10.** (2 marks, ½ each)
(a) **1-chloropropane** (chloropropane)
(b) **propanoic acid**
(c) **ethanal**
(d) **propanone**

**K11.** (2 marks)
(a) Ethanoic acid: functional group –COOH. [1]
```
      H   O
      |   ‖
  H — C — C — O — H
      |
      H
```
(b) Propanone: functional group >C=O (ketone). [1]
```
      H   O   H
      |   ‖   |
  H — C — C — C — H
      |       |
      H       H
```

**K12.** (2 marks, ½ each)
(a) **Substitution**  (b) **Addition** (hydrogenation)  (c) **Oxidation**  (d) **Combustion**

**K13.** (2 marks)
Add a little **sodium hydrogencarbonate** to each. [1]
- **Ethanoic acid:** brisk effervescence, CO2 is released (it turns limewater milky).
- **Ethanol:** no reaction, no gas. [1]

(Another valid test: ethanoic acid turns blue litmus red, and ethanol does not.)

### Short answer (3 marks)

**K14.** (3 marks)
Allotropes: **diamond, graphite and fullerene (C-60)**. [1]
**Diamond** is hard because each carbon is bonded to **four** others in a rigid three-dimensional network. [1]
**Graphite** has layers in which each carbon is bonded to **three** others, so one valence electron per carbon is **free**. This makes it a **conductor**, and the layers slide over each other, so it is soft and slippery. [1]

**K15.** (3 marks, 1 each)
(a) CH3COOH + NaOH → CH3COONa + H2O (sodium ethanoate)
(b) CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2 (sodium ethanoate)
(c) CH3COOH + C2H5OH → (conc. H2SO4, warm) CH3COOC2H5 + H2O (**ethyl ethanoate**, an ester)

**K16.** (3 marks)
A soap molecule has a **hydrophilic (ionic) head** and a **hydrophobic (hydrocarbon) tail**. [1]
In water, many soap molecules gather with their **tails pointing inward** around the oily dirt and their **heads pointing outward** into the water. This ball-shaped cluster is a **micelle**, and the dirt is trapped in its centre. [1]
The ionic heads repel each other, so the micelles stay suspended in water instead of joining up, and the dirt is washed away on rinsing. [1]
(Tails go in because they are attracted to oil and repelled by water. This is why a micelle forms.)

**K17.** (3 marks)
(a) **Catenation** is the ability of carbon atoms to link to each other to form long chains, branched chains and rings. Example: propane (C–C–C) or cyclohexane (a ring). [1]
(b) **Tetravalency**: carbon has four valence electrons and forms **four** bonds. Example: CH4, where carbon bonds to four hydrogens. [1]
Together, carbon can form chains and rings of any length and also bond with H, O, N, S and halogens, giving a very large number of compounds. [1]

**K18.** (3 marks)
**Combustion** is burning in oxygen to give CO2, water, heat and light: CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O (or C2H5OH + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 3H2O). [1]
**Oxidation** is the gain of oxygen (or loss of hydrogen) without burning, using an oxidising agent. [1]
Example: CH3CH2OH → (alkaline KMnO4, heat, or acidified K2Cr2O7) CH3COOH. [1]

**K19.** (3 marks)
**Hydrogenation** is the addition of hydrogen to an unsaturated hydrocarbon in the presence of a **nickel (or palladium)** catalyst. [1]
CH2=CH2 + H2 → (Ni) CH3–CH3 [1]
Use: converting **vegetable oils** (unsaturated) into **saturated fats (vanaspati ghee)**. [1]

**K20.** (3 marks)
(a) 2C2H5OH + 2Na → 2C2H5ONa + H2 [1]
(b) CH3CH2OH → (conc. H2SO4, 443 K) CH2=CH2 + H2O [1]
(c) The gas in (a) is **hydrogen**. A burning splinter brought near it makes it **burn with a pop sound**. [1]

### Long answer (5 marks)

**K21.** (5 marks)
(a) A **homologous series** is a family of compounds with the **same functional group** and the **same general formula**, in which successive members differ by –CH2–. [1] Two characteristics (½ each): similar chemical properties; gradual change in physical properties (such as boiling point); successive members differ by 14 u. [1]
(b) Alkanes **CnH2n+2** (methane), alkenes **CnH2n** (ethene), alkynes **CnH2n−2** (ethyne). [1]
(c) CH4 (16 u), C2H6 (30 u), C3H8 (44 u). Each member differs from the next by **–CH2–**, which is **14 u**. [1]
(d) The boiling point **increases** down the series [½], because the molecular mass grows and the forces between molecules get stronger [½]. [1]

**K22.** (5 marks)
(a) **Esterification** is the reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol, in the presence of an acid catalyst, to form an ester. [1] CH3COOH + C2H5OH → (conc. H2SO4) CH3COOC2H5 + H2O [1]
(b) **Ethyl ethanoate**, with a sweet, **fruity smell**. [1]
(c) The reaction is called **saponification**: an ester reacts with a base (NaOH) to give an alcohol and the sodium salt of the carboxylic acid. [1] CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa + C2H5OH [1]

**K23.** (5 marks)
(a) A soap is the **sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid**. [1]
(b) The soap molecule has a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail. In water the tails surround the oily dirt, with the heads facing the water, forming a **micelle** with the dirt trapped inside. [1] The micelle stays suspended in water, because the ionic heads repel each other, and it is rinsed away with the dirt. [1]
(c) In hard water, **calcium and magnesium salts** react with the soap to form an **insoluble precipitate (scum)**, so soap is wasted and the lather is poor. [1]
(d) The calcium and magnesium salts of **detergents** (sodium salts of sulphonic acids, or ammonium salts) are **soluble in water**, so no scum is formed. [1]

(ii) **Calcium (Ca²⁺) and magnesium (Mg²⁺) ions**. [1]

**R6.** (3 marks)
A **redox reaction** is one in which oxidation and reduction happen together in the same reaction. [1]
In CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O: **H2 is oxidised** (gains oxygen) and **CuO is reduced** (loses oxygen). [1]
**CuO is the oxidising agent** and **H2 is the reducing agent**. [1]

(b) The **blue colour of the solution fades** and a **reddish-brown deposit of copper** forms on the zinc. [½] Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu [1] It is a **displacement reaction**. [½]
(c) Na2SO4 + BaCl2 → BaSO4↓ (white) + 2NaCl. It is a **double displacement (precipitation) reaction**. [1]

# ACIDS, BASES AND SALTS

**A1. (d) 10** [1] A base turns red litmus blue, and bases have pH above 7.

**A2. (b) Na2CO3** [1] A salt of a strong base (NaOH) and a weak acid (H2CO3) is basic.

**A3. (a)** [1] Both true. Plaster of Paris (CaSO4·½H2O) absorbs water and turns into gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O), so moisture must be kept away.

**A4.** (2 marks)
Dry HCl gas does not ionise, so it has no H⁺ ions and does not affect dry litmus. [1]
In moist litmus, HCl dissolves in the water and gives **H⁺ (H3O⁺) ions**: HCl + H2O → H3O⁺ + Cl⁻. These ions turn blue litmus red. [1]

**A5.** (2 marks)
**Chlor-alkali process.** Products: **sodium hydroxide, chlorine and hydrogen** (2NaCl + 2H2O → 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2). [1]
Uses: NaOH for soap and paper, Cl2 for water treatment and PVC (or making bleaching powder), H2 as fuel or in making margarine. [1]

**A6.** (3 marks, 1 each)
(a) **Baking soda, NaHCO3.** Use: antacid or in baking powder.
(b) **Washing soda, Na2CO3·10H2O.** Use: washing clothes or softening hard water.
(c) **Bleaching powder, CaOCl2.** Use: disinfecting drinking water or bleaching in the textile industry.

**A7.** (5 marks)
(a) The **pH scale** runs from 0 to 14 and measures the concentration of H⁺ ions. [1] pH **below 7** is acidic, **7** is neutral, and **above 7** is basic. [1]
(b) Any three, 1 each:
- **Digestion:** the stomach makes HCl (pH about 1.5 to 3). Excess acid causes indigestion, treated with antacids.
- **Tooth decay:** bacteria make acid, and decay starts when the pH in the mouth falls below 5.5.
- **Plants and animals:** soil pH affects crops, and acid rain (pH below 5.6) harms aquatic life.
- **Self-defence:** a bee sting injects methanoic acid. Rubbing baking soda neutralises it.

**A8.** (4 marks)
(i) **Hydrogen**. [1]
(ii) It **burns with a pop sound**. [1]
(iii) Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2 [1]
(iv) Zn + 2NaOH → Na2ZnO2 + H2 (sodium zincate and hydrogen) [1]

# METALS AND NON-METALS

**M1. (b) Mercury** [1]

**M2. (c) Sodium** [1] Sodium reacts vigorously with cold water, giving NaOH and hydrogen.

**M3. (a)** [1] Both are true, and R is the definition of an amphoteric oxide, so it explains A.

**M4.** (2 marks)
Sodium and potassium are **highly reactive**. They react vigorously with oxygen and moisture in air and can even catch fire. [1]
Kerosene keeps them away from air and moisture. [1]

**M5.** (2 marks)
An **alloy** is a **homogeneous mixture of a metal with another metal or a non-metal**. [1]
Examples (any two): **brass** (copper + zinc), **bronze** (copper + tin), **solder** (lead + tin). [1]

**M6.** (3 marks)
(a) **Aluminium > zinc > iron > copper.** [1]
(b) A reaction takes place in the **zinc rod in ferrous sulphate solution**: Zn + FeSO4 → ZnSO4 + Fe. [1] Zinc is **more reactive** than iron, so it displaces it. Copper is **less reactive** than zinc, so **no reaction** occurs with zinc sulphate. [1]

**M7.** (5 marks)
(a) **Roasting:** heating a **sulphide ore** strongly in excess air: 2ZnS + 3O2 → 2ZnO + 2SO2. **Calcination:** heating a **carbonate ore** in limited or no air: ZnCO3 → ZnO + CO2. [2]
(b) ZnO + C → Zn + CO [1]
(c) By **electrolytic reduction of the molten chloride**. At the cathode: **Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na**. [1]
(d) Highly reactive metals have a **greater affinity for oxygen than carbon does**, so their oxides are more stable than carbon's and carbon cannot reduce them. [1]

**M8.** (4 marks)
(i) An **ionic bond** (electrovalent bond). [1]
(ii) Sodium (2, 8, 1) **loses one electron**: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻. Chlorine (2, 8, 7) **gains one electron**: Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻. The oppositely charged ions attract to form NaCl. [1]
(iii) The **strong electrostatic forces** between the oppositely charged ions need a lot of energy to overcome. [1]
(iv) In the **solid**, the ions are held in fixed positions and cannot move. When **molten or dissolved**, the ions move freely and carry the current. [1]
