# Social Science — Mid-Term Self-Test
### History | Geography | Civics (Political Science) | Economics — ~10 questions each, CBSE pattern

*Covers exactly the Mid-Term portions: History (Ch1 Nationalism in Europe, Ch2 Nationalism in India, Ch3 Making of a Global World — subtopics 1 to 1.3 only, Ch5 Print Culture); Geography (Ch1–6: Resources & Development, Forest & Wildlife, Water Resources, Agriculture, Minerals & Energy, Manufacturing Industries); Civics (Ch1–5: Power-sharing, Federalism, Gender/Religion/Caste, Political Parties, Outcomes of Democracy); Economics (Ch1–4: Development, Sectors of the Indian Economy, Money & Credit, Globalisation). Mix of 1-mark objective/VSA, 3-mark short-answer, and 5-mark long-answer/HOTS questions, matching the real paper's mark distribution. Attempt with a timer for realism — roughly 90 seconds per mark.*

---

## HISTORY (10 questions)

**1 mark each:**

1. Who composed the opera *Nabucco*, whose chorus became an unofficial national anthem for Italian unity?
2. Name the plebiscite-based territories where a common identity was forged partly by German customs union (Zollverein) before political unification.
3. **Assertion (A):** The Treaty of Vienna (1815) redrew European boundaries after Napoleon's defeat.
**Reason (R):** Its main objective was to undo most of the changes brought by Napoleon and restore conservative monarchies.
Choose: (a) Both A and R true, R explains A (b) Both true, R doesn't explain A (c) A true, R false (d) A false, R true.
4. Name the Ottoman Empire territory whose struggle for independence stirred pro-Greek nationalism across Europe in the 1830s.

**3 marks each:**

5. Explain any three ways in which the First World War helped spread the idea of nationalism across colonies, including India.
6. "The Salt March of 1930 was a turning point in India's freedom struggle." Justify this statement with three reasons.
7. Explain how print culture and vernacular language publishing contributed to the growth of nationalist feeling in 19th-century India (any three points).

**5 marks each:**

8. Describe, with examples, how disease and epidemics affected the "pre-modern world" (before the 19th century) in terms of trade, migration, and conquest — refer specifically to what happened in the Americas.
9. Explain the role of the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) in bringing different social groups together in the Indian freedom struggle, and why it was eventually withdrawn.
10. "Print culture created the conditions within which the French Revolution occurred." Examine this statement with suitable examples.

## GEOGRAPHY (10 questions)

**1 mark each:**

1. Which type of resource, based on ownership, includes roads, canals, and public parks?
2. Name the National Water Policy year that recommended irrigation department water rates be revised for cost recovery.
3. **Assertion (A):** Rainfed agriculture is classified into 'dryland' and 'wetland' farming.
**Reason (R):** Dryland farming is largely done in regions with less than 75 cm annual rainfall, dependent mainly on moisture retention in soil.
Choose: (a) Both true, R explains A (b) Both true, R doesn't explain A (c) A true, R false (d) A false, R true.
4. Name any one mineral classified as a "ferrous" mineral and one classified as "non-ferrous."

**3 marks each:**

5. Distinguish between renewable and non-renewable resources with two examples each, and explain why resource planning is essential for sustainable development.
6. Explain any three causes of land degradation in India.
7. Differentiate between "Rabi" and "Kharif" crops, giving two examples of each and the season they are grown in.

**5 marks each:**

8. Explain any five measures for the conservation of forest and wildlife resources in India.
9. "Water scarcity in India is not just due to the physical availability of water." Examine this statement with reference to over-exploitation and unequal access to water.
10. Describe the distribution and importance of iron ore in India, mentioning the major iron-ore-producing states/belts.

## CIVICS / POLITICAL SCIENCE (10 questions)

**1 mark each:**

1. Name the two communities between whom power-sharing arrangements were made in Belgium.
2. What is the term for a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and various constituent units of the country?
3. **Assertion (A):** In India, religion is formally separated from the State.
**Reason (R):** The Indian Constitution does not allow the government to enforce any one religion, nor to discriminate against citizens on the basis of religion.
Choose: (a) Both true, R explains A (b) Both true, R doesn't explain A (c) A true, R false (d) A false, R true.
4. Name any one national political party in India as recognised by the Election Commission.

**3 marks each:**

5. Explain the "majoritarian" measures taken by the Sri Lankan government after independence that led to the alienation of the Sri Lankan Tamils (any three).
6. Distinguish between a federal and a unitary system of government, with one example of a country following each.
7. Explain any three reasons for the growing challenges faced by political parties in India today (e.g., dynastic succession, lack of internal democracy).

**5 marks each:**

8. "Power-sharing is a good thing for a democracy, not merely a necessity." Explain this statement with reference to both the prudential and moral reasons for power-sharing.
9. Describe any five outcomes/achievements of democracy as a form of government, along with one criticism/limitation of democratic outcomes.
10. Explain the constitutional provisions that make India a federal country, and mention two features that lean toward centralisation (unitary bias).

## ECONOMICS (10 questions)

**1 mark each:**

1. Which sector of the economy includes activities like agriculture, fishing, mining, and forestry?
2. What is the term for the income of a country divided by its total population?
3. **Assertion (A):** Formal sources of credit generally charge lower interest rates than informal sources.
**Reason (R):** Formal lenders are regulated by the RBI, which oversees their lending practices and interest rates.
Choose: (a) Both true, R explains A (b) Both true, R doesn't explain A (c) A true, R false (d) A false, R true.
4. Name the international organisation that oversees rules for international trade between countries.

**3 marks each:**

5. Explain why per capita income, while useful, is not a fully sufficient indicator to compare the development of different states/countries (any three limitations).
6. Distinguish between the organised and unorganised sectors of the economy, with two examples of each.
7. Explain any three ways in which globalisation has affected Indian producers and workers (both positive and negative).

**5 marks each:**

8. Explain the difference between formal and informal sources of credit in India, and discuss why the government/RBI wants to expand formal sources of credit for the poor.
9. "There has been a shift of workers from the agricultural sector to the secondary and tertiary sectors over time in India." Explain the reasons for this shift and what it indicates about the economy.
10. Describe the role of Multi-National Companies (MNCs) in the process of globalisation, mentioning at least three ways they establish or expand production.
