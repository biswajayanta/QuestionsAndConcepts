## Mathematics

**M1. (Statistics)**
The following table shows the marks obtained by 50 students in a test, but two frequencies — x and y — are missing. The mean of the distribution is known to be 58.

| Marks | 0–20 | 20–40 | 40–60 | 60–80 | 80–100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of students | 5 | x | 10 | y | 7 |

Find the values of x and y.

**M2. (Arithmetic Progressions)**
In an AP, the sum of the 4th and 8th terms is 46, and the sum of the 6th and 10th terms is 62.
(a) Find the first term and the common difference.
(b) Hence find the sum of the first 15 terms of this AP.

**M3. (Trigonometric Identities)**
Prove that: (1 + cotθ − cosecθ)(1 + tanθ + secθ) = 2

**M4. (Quadratic Equations — word problem)**

## Physics

**P1. (Electricity — series & parallel)**
A 4 Ω resistor and a 6 Ω resistor are connected to a 12 V battery — once in series, and separately, once in parallel (assume the same 12 V battery is connected across each combination independently).
(a) Calculate the total power consumed in the series combination.
(b) Calculate the total power consumed in the parallel combination.
(c) Find the ratio of power consumed in the parallel case to the series case.

**P2. (Electricity — heating effect)**
An electric heater is rated "1100 W, 220 V."
(a) Find the current it draws, and the resistance of its heating element.
(b) If it's used for 4 hours daily and electricity costs ₹6 per unit (1 unit = 1 kWh), find the cost of running it for 30 days.
(c) Suppose the heater's element is cut into two exactly equal halves, and only one half is used (still connected to the same 220 V supply). Will the power consumed by this half-element be more, less, or the same as the original heater's power? Justify with a calculation — don't just guess.

**P3. (Magnetic Effects of Current)**
A straight current-carrying conductor is placed perpendicular to this page, with current flowing **out of the page**, toward you.
(a) Using the right-hand thumb rule, describe the direction of the magnetic field lines around the conductor (clockwise or anticlockwise, as seen by you looking at the page).
(b) A second, parallel conductor is placed nearby, also carrying current out of the page (same direction as the first). Will the two conductors attract or repel each other? Justify your answer using the idea that each conductor's magnetic field acts as an external field on the other.

**P4. (Lenses — extending magnification)**
An object 4 cm tall is placed 30 cm from a convex lens of focal length 20 cm.
(a) Find the position, nature, and size of the image formed.
(b) The object is now moved to 15 cm from the same lens. Find the new image position and nature (real/virtual, erect/inverted). Without recalculating magnification in full, explain qualitatively why the image must now be enlarged.

## Chemistry

**C1. (Metals — corrosion protection)**
Iron articles are often coated with a thin layer of zinc (galvanization) to prevent rusting.
(a) Using the reactivity series, explain why zinc protects the iron underneath **even if the coating gets scratched** and the iron is exposed to air and moisture.
(b) If a less reactive metal, like tin, were used as the coating instead of zinc, explain why a scratch would actually make rusting of the exposed iron **worse**, not better.
(c) Name this specific protective mechanism.

**C2. (Carbon Compounds)**
Two organic compounds, A and B, both have the molecular formula C₃H₈O, but are structurally different from each other. When treated with acidified potassium dichromate solution and gently heated, compound A causes the solution to change colour from orange to green. Compound B shows no such colour change even after prolonged heating.
(a) What does the orange-to-green colour change indicate about compound A? Name the functional group most likely present.
(b) Suggest why B, despite having the same molecular formula, does not show this reaction.
(c) Name compound A.

**C3. (Chemical Reactions — classification)**
Consider the (unbalanced) reaction: Fe₂O₃ + Al → Al₂O₃ + Fe
(a) Balance the equation.
(b) This reaction (used to weld railway tracks) can correctly be classified as **two different types** of reaction simultaneously. Name both, and justify each classification separately — don't just name them.

**C4. (Periodic Classification)**
An element X has atomic number 14.
(a) Using the Modern Periodic Table (arranged by atomic number), find the period and group of X, and predict whether it is a metal, non-metal, or metalloid.
(b) If X had not yet been discovered in Mendeleev's time, explain how Mendeleev would likely have predicted its properties from its position in his table. Give at least two specific properties you'd expect him to predict.

## Biology

**B1. (Heredity)**
In pea plants, tallness (T) is dominant over dwarfness (t). A tall pea plant of unknown genotype is crossed with a dwarf pea plant. Among 80 offspring, 42 are tall and 38 are dwarf (roughly a 1:1 ratio).
(a) Determine the most likely genotype of the tall parent (TT or Tt). Show the cross that supports your answer.
(b) If the tall parent had instead been TT, what ratio of tall : dwarf offspring would you expect? How would that differ from the data observed?

**B2. (Our Environment — energy flow)**
In a grassland food chain: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle, the grass fixes 100,000 kJ of solar energy per year through photosynthesis.
(a) Using the 10% law of energy transfer, calculate the energy available at each of the five trophic levels (grass through eagle).
(b) Using your actual numbers, explain why food chains rarely extend beyond 4–5 trophic levels.

**B3. (Control & Coordination — plant tropisms)**
A potted plant lying on its side near a window bends its stem upward (against gravity) and toward the light over a few days. If the same plant is instead placed lying on its side inside a completely dark box, the stem *still* bends upward against gravity, even with no light at all.
(a) Name the two types of tropism responsible for these two separate bending responses.
(b) Explain, using the role of the hormone auxin, *why* the stem bends upward even in complete darkness — describe the actual mechanism, not just the name of the tropism.

**B4. (Life Processes — excretion)**
Blood filtered at the glomerulus (in Bowman's capsule) produces a filtrate containing water, salts, urea, and glucose — essentially everything except large proteins and blood cells. But the final urine leaving the body normally contains water, salts, and urea — with essentially **no** glucose.
(a) What must happen to the glucose between initial filtration and final urine? Name the process and where in the nephron it mainly occurs.
(b) A person with untreated diabetes has very high blood glucose and often has detectable glucose in their urine, unlike a healthy person. Using your answer to (a), explain why.

## Social Science

**SST1. (History — Print Culture)**
Historians argue that the spread of the printing press in Europe wasn't just a technological change — it also helped nationalist and revolutionary ideas spread. Explain, with reasoning (not a fact list), how the ability to mass-produce printed material made it easier for such ideas to spread, compared to a time before printing existed. Refer to at least **two distinct mechanisms** (e.g. related to cost, reach, language standardisation, or accessibility).

**SST2. (Geography — Agriculture)**
Wheat is a Rabi crop (sown in winter, harvested in spring); rice is mainly a Kharif crop (sown with the monsoon, harvested in autumn).
(a) Using the specific climatic requirements of wheat and rice (temperature and water needs), explain why each is grown in its respective season rather than the other.
(b) In recent decades, irrigation has let some farmers grow rice outside the natural monsoon season. Explain how irrigation makes this possible — which specific climatic constraint does it remove?

**SST3. (Civics — Power Sharing)**
Belgium and Sri Lanka both faced ethnic/linguistic tensions after independence, but responded very differently: Belgium moved toward a federal, power-sharing system for its linguistic communities, while Sri Lanka, for a long period, pursued a centralized, majoritarian approach favouring the Sinhala-speaking majority.

Explain, using at least one specific consequence from **each** country's approach, why power-sharing arrangements are generally considered more likely to produce long-term political stability in ethnically diverse countries than majoritarian approaches.

**SST4. (Economics — Sectors of the Economy)**
In India, the agriculture (primary) sector employs a very large share of the workforce but contributes a comparatively smaller share of GDP, while the services (tertiary) sector employs a smaller share of the workforce but contributes the largest share of GDP.
(a) What does this mismatch between "share of workforce" and "share of GDP" indicate about productivity differences between the two sectors?
(b) Name and briefly explain **one** policy approach that could help address this imbalance.
