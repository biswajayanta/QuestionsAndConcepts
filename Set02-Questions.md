## Mathematics

**M1. (Trigonometry — heights & distances, complementary angles)**
From the top of a building of height h, the angles of depression of two objects lying on the same side of the building, on the ground, are found to be **complementary** (i.e., they add up to 90°). If the distances of the two objects from the foot of the building are p and q respectively, prove that:
h² = p × q

**M2. (Quadratic Equations)**
Find the value(s) of k for which the quadratic equation
2(k+1)x² − 2(k+1)x + 1 = 0
has equal roots. (Careful — check whether k+1 = 0 is itself a valid case before you finish.)

## Physics

**P1. (Human Eye)**
A person can see objects clearly only when they lie between 15 cm and 200 cm from his eye.

(a) Name the vision defect(s) this person has. Explain your reasoning using the normal near point (25 cm) and normal far point (infinity) for comparison — don't just name the defect, justify it from the numbers given.
(b) Does he need a corrective lens for **reading** at the normal reading distance of 25 cm? Explain.
(c) Find the power of the lens (if any) required to correct his **distance vision**, so that he can clearly see very distant objects.

**P2. (Electricity — series & parallel combinations)**
You have three resistors, each of resistance 6 Ω.

(a) Show how you would connect all three so that their combined (equivalent) resistance is exactly 4 Ω.
(b) What would the equivalent resistance be if all three were instead connected in parallel with each other?

## Chemistry

**C1. (Reactivity Series)**
Four metals, P, Q, R, and S, are each dipped separately into salt solutions of the other three metals, and the following is observed:
- P displaces Q and R from their salt solutions, but does **not** displace S.
- Q displaces R from its salt solution, but does **not** displace P or S.

(a) Arrange P, Q, R, and S in order of **decreasing** reactivity.
(b) Based on your ordering, predict whether S will displace P from its salt solution. Justify your answer using the reactivity series concept, not just the pattern.

**C2. (pH & Neutralisation — revisited)**
50 mL of a solution with pH 3 is mixed with 50 mL of a solution with pH 11.

(a) Will the resulting mixture **necessarily** have a pH of exactly 7? Explain your answer in terms of the number of H⁺ and OH⁻ ions present in each solution — not just the pH numbers.
(b) State the additional condition (beyond just "pH 3 and pH 11 are equally far from 7") that would guarantee the resulting mixture is exactly pH 7.

## Biology

**B1. (Reproduction in Flowering Plants)**
Flowering plants can reproduce via self-pollination (pollen transferred within the same flower/plant) or cross-pollination (pollen transferred to a different plant of the same species).

(a) Name **one** structural or functional feature that flowers commonly use to favour cross-pollination over self-pollination.
(b) Self-pollination is more "reliable," since it doesn't depend on external agents like wind, water, or insects. Despite this, explain — from an evolutionary standpoint — why cross-pollination is generally considered more advantageous for a species' long-term survival.

**B2. (Control & Coordination — hormonal vs. nervous)**
When a person is suddenly frightened, the adrenal gland releases adrenaline into the bloodstream almost instantly — increasing heart rate, redirecting blood to the muscles, and raising blood sugar, all within seconds.

(a) Hormonal (chemical) coordination is usually described as slower than nervous coordination. Explain why the adrenaline response is still fast enough to be useful in an emergency.
(b) Contrast hormonal and nervous coordination in terms of **speed** and **duration of effect** — i.e., which acts faster, and which effect tends to last longer once triggered?

## Social Science

**H1. (History — Age of Industrialisation, deeper reasoning)**
During the colonial period, the British administration did not particularly encourage Indian-owned heavy industries (such as steel or machine-tool manufacturing) — yet some Indian-owned industries, like cotton textiles and jute, were allowed to expand considerably.

Explain the likely **economic reasoning** behind this selective colonial policy. Why might a colonial power permit some kinds of Indian industry to grow while restricting others? (Think about what role India was expected to play in the British economic system — as a market, as a raw-material source, or as a producer of finished/capital goods.)

**H2. (Geography — Resources & Development)**
India has a large total land area, yet geographers describe India's usable land and resources as comparatively limited relative to its population.

Explain why simply having a large land area does **not** automatically mean a country has abundant usable resources. Refer to **at least two distinct types of constraints** on land use in your answer (for example: terrain, climate, soil quality, existing land use, or resource exhaustibility — pick any two genuinely different constraints and explain each).
