---

## How to read the "Expected answer" line under each question

Each question carries an *Expected answer* line that tells you the length, and whether a diagram, equation, definition or name is needed. Use it to structure the answer and to check the format afterwards.

| Marks | Typical length | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| MCQ / Assertion-Reason (1) | Option letter only | No working is marked. Write (a), (b), (c) or (d). |
| Very short (2) | 2 to 4 lines, 30 to 50 words | One mark per correct point. Do not write an essay. |
| Short (3) | 5 to 7 lines, 50 to 80 words | Three clear points, or two points and a diagram or equation. |
| Long (5) | 10 to 15 lines, 100 to 150 words | Answer each lettered part separately, with its own mark. |
| Case-based (4) | 1 to 2 lines per part | Answer only what each part asks. |

**Always:** equations balanced, with conditions and the catalyst on the arrow; diagrams labelled, drawn with pencil and ruler; definitions in one sentence.

# CARBON AND ITS COMPOUNDS (K1 to K26)

### MCQ (1 mark each)

**K1.** The number of covalent bonds in a molecule of propane (C3H8) is:
(a) 8  (b) 9  (c) 10  (d) 11
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**K2.** Which of the following is an unsaturated hydrocarbon?
(a) C2H6  (b) C3H8  (c) C2H4  (d) CH4
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**K3.** The IUPAC name of CH3CH2CH2OH is:
(a) ethanol  (b) propanol  (c) propanal  (d) propanone
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**K4.** Which of the following is **not** a property of covalent compounds?
(a) Low melting and boiling points
(b) Poor conductors of electricity
(c) Generally soluble in organic solvents
(d) Conduct electricity in the molten state
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**K5.** Ethanol heated with excess concentrated H2SO4 at 443 K gives:
(a) ethane  (b) ethene  (c) ethanoic acid  (d) ethyl ethanoate
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

### Assertion-Reason (1 mark each)

**K6.** **Assertion (A):** Carbon forms a very large number of compounds.
**Reason (R):** Carbon shows catenation and tetravalency.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**K7.** **Assertion (A):** Soaps are effective cleansing agents in hard water.
**Reason (R):** Soap molecules form micelles in water.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

### Very short answer (2 marks each)

**K8.** Draw the electron dot structure of methane (CH4). Why does carbon form covalent bonds instead of ionic bonds?
*Expected answer: a drawn electron dot structure (dots and crosses; carbon with an octet, each hydrogen with a duet) [1], plus a 1 to 2 line reason [1]. About 3 lines and a diagram.*

**K9.** What is a homologous series? Write the molecular formulae of the first four members of the alkane series.
*Expected answer: a one-sentence definition naming the three features (same functional group, same general formula, successive members differ by –CH2–) [1], and four formulae in one line [1]. About 3 lines.*

**K10.** Write the IUPAC name of each: (a) CH3CH2CH2Cl  (b) CH3CH2COOH  (c) CH3CHO  (d) CH3COCH3
*Expected answer: four names, one line each. No explanation needed.*

**K11.** Draw the structural formulae of (a) ethanoic acid and (b) propanone, and mark the functional group in each.
*Expected answer: two full structural formulae with every bond drawn, and the functional group circled or labelled in each. Diagram required; no text beyond labels.*

**K12.** Name the type of reaction in each case:
(a) CH4 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + HCl (in sunlight)
(b) CH2=CH2 + H2 → CH3–CH3 (Ni catalyst)
(c) CH3CH2OH → CH3COOH (with alkaline KMnO4)
(d) CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O
*Expected answer: one or two words for each part.*

**K13.** How would you distinguish ethanol from ethanoic acid by a chemical test? State the observation in each case.
*Expected answer: name the reagent and the observation for each compound [1 + 1]. 3 to 4 lines. An equation is optional.*

### Short answer (3 marks each)

**K14.** Name three allotropes of carbon. Explain why diamond is very hard whereas graphite is soft and conducts electricity.
*Expected answer: three names [1]; the structure of diamond (each carbon bonded to four others, rigid 3-D network) [1]; the structure of graphite (layers, three bonds per carbon, one free electron) [1]. 5 to 7 lines. A diagram is optional.*

**K15.** Write balanced chemical equations for the reaction of ethanoic acid with: (a) sodium hydroxide  (b) sodium hydrogencarbonate  (c) ethanol in the presence of conc. H2SO4. Name the organic product wherever one is formed.
*Expected answer: three balanced equations, with the catalyst on the arrow in (c), and the name of each organic product written next to its equation. About 4 lines, no explanation.*

**K16.** Explain the cleansing action of soap. Why do soap molecules form micelles in water?
*Expected answer: 6 to 7 lines. Mention hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tail, tails towards the oil and heads towards the water, the micelle trapping the dirt, and rinsing away. A labelled micelle diagram (head, tail, dirt) is strongly expected and earns marks.*

**K17.** What is meant by (a) catenation and (b) tetravalency? Give one example of each, and explain how together they lead to the very large number of carbon compounds.
*Expected answer: a one-line definition and one example for each [1 + 1], and 1 to 2 lines linking them [1]. 5 to 6 lines. Formulae or a small structure may be given as the example.*

**K18.** Distinguish between combustion and oxidation of carbon compounds. Write one equation for each, with the reagent needed for the oxidation.
*Expected answer: a one-line definition of each [1 + 1] and two equations. The oxidation equation must show the reagent on the arrow. About 5 lines.*

**K19.** What is hydrogenation? Write the equation for the hydrogenation of ethene with the catalyst used, and state one industrial use of the process.
*Expected answer: a one-line definition, one equation with Ni on the arrow, and one use. About 4 to 5 lines.*

**K20.** Write balanced equations for: (a) ethanol reacting with sodium  (b) ethanol heated with excess conc. H2SO4 at 443 K. Name the gas evolved in (a) and give a test for it.
*Expected answer: two balanced equations (conditions on the arrow in (b)), the name of the gas, and a one-line test with its observation. About 4 to 5 lines.*

*Expected answer: 10 to 12 lines in all. (a) a one-line definition and a balanced equation with conc. H2SO4 on the arrow. (b) one line. (c) the name, a one-line definition and a balanced equation. No diagram needed.*

**K23.**
(a) What is a soap, chemically? **[1]**

### Case-based (4 marks each)

**K24.** A student studies four hydrocarbons: methane (CH4), ethane (C2H6), ethene (C2H4) and ethyne (C2H2).
(i) Which of these are saturated hydrocarbons? **[1]**
(ii) Write the general formula of the alkene series. **[1]**
(iii) Which of these undergo addition reactions? **[1]**
(iv) Write the equation for the addition reaction of ethene with hydrogen, with the catalyst. **[1]**
*Expected answer: (i) to (iii) one line each. (iv) one balanced equation with Ni on the arrow.*

**K25.** A teacher heats 1 mL of ethanol and 1 mL of glacial ethanoic acid with a few drops of conc. H2SO4 in a test tube in a warm water bath. She then pours the mixture into a beaker of cold water and the class notices a sweet, fruity smell.

# CHEMICAL REACTIONS AND EQUATIONS (R1 to R8)

**R1. (1 mark)** Which of the following is a decomposition reaction?
(a) CaCO3 → CaO + CO2  (b) Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu  (c) NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H2O  (d) 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**R2. (1 mark)** In the reaction Fe2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Fe, the substance that is oxidised is:
(a) Fe2O3  (b) Al  (c) Al2O3  (d) Fe
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**R3. (1 mark)** **Assertion (A):** Silver chloride turns grey in sunlight.
**Reason (R):** Silver chloride decomposes to silver and chlorine in the presence of sunlight.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**R4. (2 marks)** Why should a magnesium ribbon be cleaned before it is burnt in air? Write the equation for the reaction.
*Expected answer: a 1 to 2 line reason [1] and one balanced equation [1]. About 3 lines.*

**R5. (2 marks)** Balance the following equations:
(a) Fe + H2O → Fe3O4 + H2
(b) BaCl2 + Al2(SO4)3 → AlCl3 + BaSO4
*Expected answer: two balanced equations only, written with the coefficients. No explanation needed.*

**R6. (3 marks)** What is a redox reaction? In the reaction CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, identify the substance oxidised, the substance reduced, the oxidising agent and the reducing agent.
*Expected answer: a one-line definition [1], and four one-word or one-line identifications [2]. About 4 to 5 lines.*

**R7. (5 marks)**
(a) With one balanced equation each, explain thermal decomposition and photolytic decomposition. **[2]**
(b) What is observed when a zinc granule is added to copper sulphate solution? Write the equation and name the type of reaction. **[2]**
(c) Write the equation for a reaction in which a white precipitate forms, and name the type of reaction. **[1]**
*Expected answer: 8 to 10 lines in all. (a) a one-line meaning of each type, with its equation and the energy shown on the arrow. (b) the colour change of the solution and the deposit, a balanced equation and the type. (c) one equation and the type of reaction.*

**R8. (4 marks, case-based)** A student heats lead nitrate powder strongly in a dry boiling tube. Brown fumes come out and a residue remains in the tube.
(i) Name the brown gas. **[1]**
(ii) Name the type of reaction. **[1]**
(iii) Write the balanced chemical equation. **[1]**
(iv) Name the other two products. **[1]**

**A1. (1 mark)** A solution turns red litmus paper blue. Its pH is likely to be:
(a) 2  (b) 4  (c) 5  (d) 10

**A2. (1 mark)** Which of the following salt solutions has a pH greater than 7?
(a) NaCl  (b) Na2CO3  (c) NH4Cl  (d) KNO3
*Expected answer: option letter only.*

**A3. (1 mark)** **Assertion (A):** Plaster of Paris should be stored in a moisture-proof container.
**Reason (R):** Plaster of Paris absorbs water and sets to a hard mass of gypsum.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

**A4. (2 marks)** Dry HCl gas does not change the colour of dry blue litmus paper, but moist blue litmus paper turns red. Explain.
*Expected answer: 3 to 4 lines. Name the ions responsible (H⁺ or H3O⁺) and say when they form. An ionisation equation is optional but welcome.*

**A5. (2 marks)** Name the process in which a solution of sodium chloride is electrolysed. Name its three products and give one use of each.
*Expected answer: the name of the process [½], the three products [½], and one use for each [1]. A table or 4 lines. The overall equation is optional.*

**A6. (3 marks)** Write the name, the chemical formula and one use of each: (a) baking soda  (b) washing soda  (c) bleaching powder.
*Expected answer: a small table with three rows (chemical name, formula, one use). No paragraph needed.*

**A7. (5 marks)**

(b) Explain three ways in which pH is important in everyday life. **[3]**
*Expected answer: 10 to 12 lines in all. (a) a one-line definition and three values with their meaning. A labelled 0 to 14 scale is a good addition. (b) three separate points of 1 to 2 lines each, each with a specific example (such as stomach acid, tooth decay or soil).*

**A8. (4 marks, case-based)** A student puts zinc granules in a test tube and adds dilute hydrochloric acid. A gas is evolved. She brings a burning splinter near the mouth of the tube. In a second test tube, she warms zinc granules with sodium hydroxide solution and a gas is evolved again.
(i) Name the gas. **[1]**
(ii) What is observed with the burning splinter? **[1]**
(iii) Write the equation for the reaction with dilute HCl. **[1]**
(iv) Write the equation for the reaction with sodium hydroxide. **[1]**
*Expected answer: (i) and (ii) one line each. (iii) and (iv) balanced equations.*

# METALS AND NON-METALS (M1 to M8)
