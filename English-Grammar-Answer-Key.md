## UNIT 1 — DETERMINERS

**A.**
1. **an** — "honest" starts with a vowel *sound* (silent h).
2. **much** — "information" is uncountable.
3. **a few** — positive sense ("some are left, hurry to grab them").
4. **few** — negative sense (he has almost none, hence lonely).
5. **Each** — emphasizes individuality of every student submitting separately.
6. **Each** — used for exactly two ("the two brothers").
7. **The, the** — "sun" and "east" are unique/specific things.
8. **a** — "European" starts with a "yoo" (consonant) sound, not a vowel sound.
9. **much** — "water" is uncountable.
10. **an, the** — "an umbrella" (first mention, vowel sound), "the umbrella" (already specified).
11. **Many** — "children" is countable plural.
12. **a little** — positive sense (some genuinely useful advice), "advice" is uncountable.
13. **An** — "hour" starts with a silent h → vowel sound.
14. **Every** — used for a larger group (implies more than two, "not just some").
15. **a little** — positive("some", though barely enough), "money" is uncountable.

**B.**
16. **(c) A is false, but R is true.** "Few" (negative — almost none) and "a few" (positive — some) do NOT mean the same thing, even though the reasoning about them both being countable-plural in some general sense is on the right track for R being considered true in isolation — but the correct emphasis is: A is false because they have opposite connotations.
17. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.** This is exactly the sound-based rule.
18. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.** "Each" is valid for two or more, and does emphasize individuality.

## UNIT 2 — TENSES

**A.**
1. **rises** — simple present, general truth.
2. **are playing** — present continuous, happening right now.
3. **has finished** — present perfect, recent completion with present relevance (can go out now).
4. **has lived** — present perfect, started in past and continues to now.
5. **are leaving** — present continuous used for planned near future.
6. **had already left** — past perfect, this action happened before another past action (my reaching the station).
7. **was cooking** — past continuous, ongoing action interrupted by the phone ringing.
8. **have not seen** — present perfect with "since" is wrong here; actually correct answer is **haven't seen** (present perfect, unspecified/ongoing time reference "since last month" is valid with present perfect — note: "since" pairs correctly with present perfect, so this is right as "have not seen").
9. **went, bought** — simple past, both completed actions at a definite past time (yesterday).
10. **had finished** — past perfect, cooking was completed before the guests (another past event) arrived.
11. **has studied** — present perfect (or present perfect continuous "has been studying"), action started in past, continues now, with "for five years."
12. **met** — simple past, specific past time marker ("last week").

**B.**
13. **(b) I have seen that movie already.** — present perfect, correctly formed with "have + past participle."
14. **(b) She had left before he arrived.** — past perfect ("had left") for the earlier action, simple past ("arrived") for the later one.
15. **(c) I have lived here since 2010.** — "since" is used with a specific point in time; "for" would be used with a duration (e.g., "for 15 years").

**C.**
16. **(b) A is false, but R is true, and R explains why A is false.** They are NOT interchangeable in all contexts — R correctly explains why (different time-reference requirements).
17. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.**
18. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.**

## UNIT 3 — MODALS

**A.**
1. **must** — strong obligation, a legal rule.
2. **need not** — absence of obligation, optional.
3. **must not** — strict prohibition.
4. **May** — asking permission, polite/formal.
5. **might** — possibility, less certain ("a bit cloudy" suggests uncertainty).
6. **could** — past general ability.
7. **should** — advice/moral obligation (good manners).
8. **have** ("have to") — obligation from an external rule (company deadline), not personal opinion.
9. **Would** — polite request.
10. **can** — present ability.

**B.**
11. **(b) You need not go.** — means it's not necessary, but not forbidden either.
12. **(b) You must not smoke here.** — expresses prohibition.
13. **(b) I must finish this task tonight, I feel it's important.** — "must" here reflects the speaker's own sense of importance/obligation, not an external rule (unlike "have to"/"need to" in the other options).

**C.**
14. **(b) A is false, but R is true, and R explains why A is false.** "Must not" (forbidden) and "need not" (not required, but permitted) are very different — A is false, and R correctly explains the distinction.
15. **(b) A is true, but R is false.** A is correct — "have to" often signals external obligation while "must" often signals the speaker's own judgment. But R is false — they do NOT always refer to the same source of obligation; that's precisely the distinction A describes.
16. **(b) A is true, but R is false.** A is correct about degree of certainty. R is false — "might" can express present possibility too; it is not merely a "past tense of may" in modern usage (both "may" and "might" can refer to present/future possibility, with "might" indicating lower likelihood).

**D.**
17. **Error:** "needn't to worry" → **Correction:** "needn't worry" (modals are followed directly by the base verb, no "to").
18. **Error:** "can able to solve" → **Correction:** "is able to solve" or simply "can solve" (never combine "can" with "able to" — that's a double modal-like structure and incorrect).

## UNIT 4 — SUBJECT–VERB CONCORD

**A.**
1. **has** — "each" is always singular, even though "of the students" sounds plural.
2. **is** — "team" as a single unit (playing together).
3. **were** — "neither...nor" agreement matches the nearer subject, "employees" (plural).
4. **was** — "along with his teammates" is an inserted phrase; the real subject is "the captain" (singular).
5. **were** — "a number of" (meaning "many") takes a plural verb.
6. **is** — "the number of" (a specific count) takes a singular verb.
7. **was** — "everyone" is an indefinite pronoun, always singular.
8. **is** — "bread and butter" is treated as a single dish/concept.
9. **are** — "Ram and Shyam" are two distinct people joined by "and" → plural verb.
10. **is** — "neither" (of the answers) is always singular.
11. **is** — "either...or" agreement matches the nearer subject, "the teacher" (singular).
12. **has** — "jury" as a single unit reaching one unanimous verdict.
13. **is** — "as well as my parents" is an inserted phrase; the real subject is "my brother" (singular).
14. **knows** — "nobody" is an indefinite pronoun, always singular.
15. **was** — "the news" is the actual subject (singular, uncountable-like usage), not "success."

**B.**
16. **Error:** "The list of items are on the table." → **Correction:** "The list of items **is** on the table." (Real subject is "the list," singular; "of items" is a prepositional phrase, not the subject.)
17. **Error:** "Either of the two roads lead to the station." → **Correction:** "Either of the two roads **leads** to the station." ("Either" is always singular.)
18. **Correct as is** if intending the committee as a single body acting together, "has decided" would be the more standard CBSE-preferred form (collective noun as one unit) — flag "have" as acceptable only if emphasizing the members individually, but for board exams, prefer: "The committee **has** decided..."

**C.**
19. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.**
20. **(b) A is true, but R is false — the verb agrees with the nearer subject, not the first one.** The correct rule is that the verb agrees with the subject *nearer* to it, not the first-mentioned one — R states the rule backwards, so R is false even though A (the specific example) is correctly analysed.

## UNIT 5 — REPORTED SPEECH

**A.**
1. He said (that) he **was going** to the market. *(Present continuous → Past continuous; "I" → "he")*
2. She said (that) she **had completed** her project. *(Present perfect → Past perfect)*
3. The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun. *(No backshift — universal truth stays in present tense even after "said")*
4. He **told me** (that) he **would help** me the next day. *("said to me" → "told me"; "will" → "would"; "tomorrow" → "the next day")*
5. She said (that) she **had been reading** a novel when I **called**. *(Past continuous → Past perfect continuous; the "when you called" clause also shifts to match, and "you" → "I" from her perspective — note: exact pronoun shift depends on who is reporting)*
6. He **asked if/whether** I **was** coming to the party. *(Yes/No question → "if/whether" + statement word order; "are" → "was")*
7. She **asked me if/whether** I **liked** ice cream. *(Yes/No question; "do you like" → "I liked," no auxiliary "do" in reported clause)*
8. The teacher **asked** the students **where they lived**. *(Wh-question → no "if/whether," just the wh-word directly; "do you live" → "they lived")*
9. He **asked her what her name was**. *(Wh-question; note the word order — "what her name was," NOT "what was her name")*
10. She **asked why I/she was** late that day. *(Wh-question; "today" → "that day")*
11. The officer **ordered/told them to sit down**. *(Command → "to + infinitive")*
12. The mother **told/warned her child not to touch** that wire. *(Negative command → "not to + infinitive")*
13. He **requested the waiter to bring** him a glass of water. *(Polite request → "requested + object + to + infinitive")*
14. The teacher **told/instructed** the students **to submit** their assignments on time. *(Command/instruction → "to + infinitive")*
15. She **requested them not to shout** in the library. *(Negative request → "not to + infinitive")*

**B.**
16. **Error:** "He asked that where was I going." → **Correction:** "He asked where I was going." (No "that" with wh-questions; statement word order — "I was going," not "was I going.")
17. **Error:** "She asked if am I free the next day." → **Correction:** "She asked if I was free the next day." (Statement word order — "I was," not "am I"; also tense backshift "are" → "was.")
18. **Error:** "The teacher said do your homework daily." → **Correction:** "The teacher told them to do their homework daily." (Commands need "told + object + to + infinitive," not the plain imperative repeated as-is.)

**C.**
19. **(a) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.**
20. **(b) A is true, but R is false.** A correctly describes the "to + infinitive" rule. R is false — commands do NOT retain their exact imperative form in reported speech; that's precisely why the "to + infinitive" transformation (described in A) is needed.

## Quick scoring guide for you

- Units 1–4: straightforward right/wrong against the key above.
- Unit 5 (Reported Speech) is the highest-value, most heavily tested unit — if he's shaky anywhere, prioritize re-doing Section A (1–15) out loud with him, since the actual board exam almost always tests this as a "rewrite the dialogue/passage in reported speech" integrated question, not standalone MCQs.
- The assertion-reason questions are less common in the actual CBSE English paper (they're more typical of Science/SST), but useful here purely as a concept-check — if he gets the *reasoning* questions right, it confirms he understands the rule rather than having memorized an example.
