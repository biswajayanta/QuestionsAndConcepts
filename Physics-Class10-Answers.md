# MAGNETIC EFFECTS OF ELECTRIC CURRENT

### MCQ and Assertion-Reason

| Q | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| MG1 | **(b)** | Outside the magnet the lines go from north to south. They close up inside, running south to north. |
| MG2 | **(c)** | The field grows with the current and falls with distance. Reversing the current reverses the direction, not the strength. |
| MG3 | **(a)** | Forefinger = field, central finger = current, thumb = force. |
| MG4 | **(b)** | Both statements are true. R does not explain A. The reason lines never cross is that, at the crossing point, the field would have two directions. |
| MG5 | **(d)** | A is false. A frequency of 50 Hz means 50 complete cycles per second, and the direction reverses twice in each cycle, so **100 times per second** (every 1/100 s). R is true. |

### Very short answer (2 marks)

**MG6.** (2 marks)
**The diagram must show** [1]: a bar magnet with N and S marked; closed curves of field lines; arrows pointing **from N to S outside** the magnet and from **S to N inside**; lines closer together near the poles.
Any two properties (½ each) [1]:
- Outside the magnet the lines emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole.
- They are closed curves.
- They never intersect.
- Where they are closer together, the field is stronger.
- The tangent to a line gives the direction of the field at that point.

**MG7.** (2 marks)
**Right-hand thumb rule:** imagine holding the straight current-carrying wire in the right hand with the thumb pointing in the direction of the current. The fingers then wrap round the wire in the direction of the magnetic field lines. [1]
For a current flowing upward, the field lines circle the wire **anticlockwise** when seen from above (the thumb points up, towards the viewer, and the fingers curl anticlockwise). [1]

### Short answer (3 marks)

**MG8.** (3 marks)
**The diagram must show** [1]: a coil of several turns with the current direction marked (or the battery); parallel straight lines inside the coil; lines curving round outside from one end to the other, like a bar magnet; arrows on the lines; one end marked N and the other S.
Inside the solenoid the field lines are **parallel straight lines**, so the field is **uniform**: the same strength and direction at every point inside. [1]
Any two ways to increase the strength (½ each) [1]: increase the **current**; increase the **number of turns**; insert a **soft iron core**.

**MG9.** (3 marks)
(a) **Fleming's left-hand rule:** stretch the thumb, forefinger and central finger of the left hand so that they are **mutually perpendicular**. If the forefinger points in the direction of the **magnetic field** and the central finger in the direction of the **current**, then the thumb points in the direction of the **force** on the conductor. [1]
(b) Field downward (forefinger down) and current east (central finger east) gives the thumb pointing **north**. The force acts **towards the north**. [1]
(c) Reversing the current reverses the force. The force acts **towards the south**. [1]

### Long answer (5 marks)

**MG10.** (5 marks)
(a) **Live wire (red insulation)**, **neutral wire (black insulation)**, **earth wire (green insulation)**. [1]
(b) The earth wire is connected to the **metal body** of the appliance and to a metal plate deep in the earth. If the body becomes live because of a fault, the leaking current flows to the earth through this **low-resistance path**. The body stays at the potential of the earth, so the user **does not get a shock**. [1]
(c) **Short-circuiting** happens when the live wire and the neutral wire come into direct contact (for example, through damaged insulation). The resistance becomes very small, so a **very large current** flows. [1]
(d) A **fuse** is a piece of wire made of a metal or alloy with an **appropriately low melting point**, connected **in series** in the circuit. [1] When the current becomes larger than the safe value (short circuit or overloading), the fuse wire gets heated by the **heating effect of current**, **melts**, and **breaks the circuit**, so the appliance is not damaged. [1]

### Case-based (4 marks)

**MG11.** (4 marks)
(i) An **electromagnet**. [1]
(ii) The soft iron core stays magnetised only while current flows through the coil. When the switch is opened, the current stops, the field disappears, and the nail **loses its magnetism**, so the pins fall. [1]
(iii) Any two (½ each) [1]: increase the **number of turns** of the coil; increase the **current** (use more cells); use a larger or better soft iron core.
(iv) The **direction of the magnetic field reverses**, so the two ends of the nail **interchange their north and south poles**. [1]

**MG12.** (4 marks)
(i) **Hans Christian Oersted**. [1]
(ii) **Concentric circles** around the wire. [1]
(iii) The compass needle **deflects in the opposite direction**. This shows that the **direction of the magnetic field depends on the direction of the current**. [1]
(iv) The field becomes **weaker** as the distance from the wire increases. [1]

# ELECTRICITY

**EL1. (b) R** [1]: R′ = ρ(2l)/(2A) = ρl/A = R.

**EL2. (c)** [1]: A is true and R is false. Copper has a **low** resistivity, and that is why it is used for wires.

**EL3.** (2 marks)
**Resistivity** is the resistance of a conductor of **unit length and unit area of cross-section**. [1]
SI unit: **ohm metre (Ω m)**. Alloy with high resistivity (any one): nichrome, manganin or constantan. [1]

**EL4.** (3 marks)
(a) 4 Ω and 6 Ω in parallel: 1/R = 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12, so R = 12/5 = **2.4 Ω**. [½] Total R = 2.4 + 1.6 = **4.0 Ω**. [½]
(b) I = V/R = 12 / 4.0 = **3 A**. [1]
(c) p.d. across the parallel combination = I × 2.4 = 3 × 2.4 = **7.2 V**. [½] Current through the 6 Ω resistor = 7.2 / 6 = **1.2 A**. [½]
(The current through the 4 Ω resistor is 1.8 A, and 1.8 + 1.2 = 3 A.)

**EL5.** (3 marks)
(a) I = P/V = 1100 / 220 = **5 A**. [1]
(b) R = V/I = 220 / 5 = **44 Ω** (or R = V²/P). [1]
(c) Energy = P × t = 1.1 kW × 2 h = **2.2 kWh**. [½] Cost = 2.2 × ₹5 = **₹11**. [½]

**EL6.** (5 marks)
(a) **Ohm's law:** at constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is **directly proportional to the potential difference** across its ends (V = IR). [1]
(b) **The circuit diagram must show** [2]: a battery or cell, a key, the resistor, a rheostat, an **ammeter connected in series** with the resistor and a **voltmeter connected in parallel** across it, using standard symbols.
(c) A **straight line passing through the origin**, with V on the x-axis and I on the y-axis (or the other way round, with the axes labelled). The slope of a V against I graph gives the **resistance R**. [1]
(d) **Joule's law:** the heat produced in a resistor is directly proportional to the square of the current, to the resistance, and to the time for which the current flows: **H = I²Rt**. [1]

**EL7.** (4 marks)
(i) **Silver** (lowest resistivity). [1]
(ii) Nichrome has a **high resistivity** and a **high melting point**, and it does **not oxidise (burn) readily** when it is red hot. [1]
(iii) Copper has a **very low resistivity**, so the wires have a low resistance and little heat is lost. [1]
(iv) R = ρl/A. With the same l and A, R is proportional to ρ. R(nichrome) / R(silver) = 100 / 1.60 = **62.5**. The nichrome wire has **62.5 times** the resistance of the silver wire. [1]

# LIGHT: REFLECTION AND REFRACTION

**LR1. (c) 30 cm** [1]: an image the same size as the object forms when the object is at C. C is at 2f = 30 cm.

**LR2. (a) 2 × 10⁸ m/s** [1]: v = c/n = 3 × 10⁸ / 1.5.

**LR3. (a)** [1]: both statements are true. R explains A, since P = 1/f, and f is negative for a concave lens.

**LR4.** (2 marks)
**The diagram must show** [1]: a concave mirror with P, F and C marked; an object between P and F; two rays (one parallel to the axis that reflects through F, and one that falls along the normal, so that its backward extension passes through C, and is reflected back along itself) with arrows; the reflected rays diverging, and their dotted extensions meeting behind the mirror; the image labelled.
Image is **virtual, erect and enlarged** (any two) and formed behind the mirror. [1]

**LR5.** (3 marks)
u = −20 cm, f = +10 cm, h = +4 cm.
(a) 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/10 − 1/20 = 1/20, so v = **+20 cm**. The image is on the other side of the lens, 20 cm from it. [1]
(b) m = v/u = 20 / (−20) = **−1**. [1]
(c) h′ = m × h = −1 × 4 = **−4 cm**. The image is **4 cm high, real and inverted** (the same size as the object). [1]

**LR6.** (5 marks)
(a) (i) The **incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal** at the point of incidence all lie in the **same plane**. [1]
(ii) **Snell's law:** the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is a **constant** for a given colour of light and a given pair of media (sin i / sin r = constant). [1]
(b) The **refractive index** of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (or air) to the speed of light in the medium: n = c/v. [1]
v = c/n = 3 × 10⁸ / 1.33 = **2.26 × 10⁸ m/s**. [1]
(c) **The diagram must show** [1]: a rectangular glass slab; the incident ray bending **towards** the normal on entering; the ray bending **away** from the normal on leaving; the **emergent ray parallel to the incident ray**; the lateral displacement marked; normals and angles i, r and e labelled; arrows.

**LR7.** (4 marks)
(i) A convex mirror gives a **wider field of view**, and it always forms an **erect** image of the traffic behind. [1]
(ii) **Virtual, erect and diminished.** [1]
(iii) u = −20 cm, f = +20 cm. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/20 + 1/20 = 1/10, so v = **+10 cm** (behind the mirror). [1]
(iv) m = −v/u = −(10) / (−20) = **+0.5**. [1]

# THE HUMAN EYE AND THE COLOURFUL WORLD

**HE1. (b)** [1]: in hypermetropia the near point is far away, and the image of a nearby object forms **behind** the retina. A convex lens corrects it.

**HE2.** (2 marks)
The **Tyndall effect** is the **scattering of light by colloidal particles** (or fine particles in a medium), which makes the path of a beam of light visible. [1]
The sky appears blue because the molecules of air and other fine particles scatter the **shorter wavelengths (blue)** much more strongly than the longer wavelengths (red). [1]

**HE3.** (3 marks)
**Dispersion** is the splitting of white light into its component colours when it passes through a prism. [1]
**The diagram must show** [1]: a triangular glass prism; a ray of white light incident on one face; the emergent beam spread into a spectrum on a screen; **violet at the bottom (deviated most)** and **red at the top (deviated least)** labelled (as the prism is usually drawn with its base at the bottom).
Colours in order: **violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red** (VIBGYOR). [1]

**HE4.** (4 marks)
(i) **Myopia** (short-sightedness). [1]
(ii) A **concave lens**. [1]
(iii) f = 1/P = 1 / (−2.5) = −0.4 m = **−40 cm**. [1]
(iv) **In front of the retina.** [1]
